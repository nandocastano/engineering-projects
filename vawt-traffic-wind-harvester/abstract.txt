Harvesting Energy from Traffic-Induced Wind: A Compact Vertical-Axis Turbine with a Carbon-Fibre Rotor

Fast highway traffic leaves a turbulent wake that is not harvested. For our NYU Abu Dhabi engineering capstone we designed, simulated, built and tested a small vertical-axis wind turbine for roadside use, and developed an all-composite carbon-fibre rotor made entirely by hand layup: blades laid up in PLA-printed moulds, a tube wrapped on a mandrel, and a hub plate cut from a flat laminate. The number of plies came from flexural bending tests of coupons and a static finite-element study of the hub driven by loads from our own per-revolution aerodynamic model. In side-by-side logged records the carbon-fibre prototype gave about 3.2x the mean output voltage of the first PLA prototype (about 6.8 V vs 2.1 V) and stayed above 2 V for about 88% of the record versus 42%. We also built the self-contained data logger (divider, SD card, change-triggered logging) used for all measurements. The records are voltage, not power, and were taken in separate sessions under different wind; load-based power validation, fatigue, noise and UV/thermal tests have not been done. Aerodynamic profile definitions, rotor dimensions and laminate schedules are withheld because of IP and patent filing.

Román I. Villarreal, Juan Fernando Castaño, Larissa Al Kseiri. Supervisors: P. George, J. Teo, J. I. Ryu. NYU Abu Dhabi, May 2025.
